# Module I — Question bank

Engineering and Engineer: discipline and profession, attributes and functions, problem solver / designer / change agent.

## 1-mark questions

### Engineering as a discipline and a profession

**1. Define engineering.**
A professional discipline that combines knowledge from physics, mathematics and material science with innovation, analysis and design to develop technologies and solutions meeting human needs in a safe, efficient and sustainable way.

**2. What do you understand by engineering as a discipline?**
A branch of academic study and systematic knowledge that applies scientific and mathematical principles to solve real-world problems, focusing on problem-solving and innovation.

**3. What is engineering as a profession?**
The practical application of engineering knowledge to design, develop, operate and maintain systems, products and processes that serve society, done responsibly, ethically and efficiently.

**4. Name the six components of engineering as a discipline.**
Scientific foundation, mathematical modelling, design thinking, technical knowledge, experimentation and analysis, innovation and research.

**5. Name the six elements of engineering as a profession.**
Application of knowledge, professional responsibility, ethics and integrity, licence and certification, teamwork and communication, lifelong learning.

**6. Give any three characteristics of engineering as a discipline.**
Interdisciplinary nature, a strong foundation in mathematics and science, and emphasis on continual improvement and lifelong learning. (Also involves design, analysis and experimentation.)

**7. Name any three key branches of engineering.**
Mechanical, Electrical, Civil. (Also Chemical, Computer.)

**8. Expand PE and state who needs it.**
Professional Engineer. In the US, a civil engineer needs a PE licence to approve structural designs for public infrastructure such as bridges or highways.

**9. What is a Chartered Engineer?**
An engineer registered with the Institution of Engineers (India), whose certification validates professional competence and allows them to sign technical documents for bodies such as CPWD, Indian Railways and NHAI.

**10. Name any two professional bodies that regulate engineering.**
IEEE and ASCE. (Also NEC, IEI in India.)

**11. What is meant by mathematical modelling in engineering?**
Using equations and formulas to model systems, predict outcomes and optimise performance. Mathematics is the language of engineering.

**12. Give one example of professional responsibility in engineering.**
A structural engineer must ensure a building is safe, sustainable and compliant with regulations, since lives depend on it.

**13. Give one example of ethics and integrity in practice.**
An engineer must refuse to certify a faulty design, even under management pressure, if it compromises safety.

**14. Why is lifelong learning an element of the profession?**
Technology and industry needs evolve constantly, so engineers must continuously upgrade skills, tools and methods. Example: an electrical engineer learning AI and IoT for smart grids.

**15. State the goal of the discipline versus the profession.**
The discipline aims to advance the frontiers of science and technology. The profession aims to ensure safety, efficiency and service to society.

**16. State the output of the discipline versus the profession.**
Discipline: scientific theories, experimental results, conceptual designs. Profession: functional products, infrastructure, processes and services.

### Attributes and functions of a practicing engineer

**17. Who is a practicing engineer?**
Someone who applies engineering principles to solve real-world problems and is actively involved in the profession, either as a licensed PE or as someone gaining experience under a PE's supervision.

**18. State the scope of the practicing engineer's role.**
From concept to reality, transforming ideas into practical solutions.

**19. Name the six key attributes of a practicing engineer.**
Technical proficiency, ethical integrity, problem-solving skills, communication skills, teamwork and collaboration, adaptability and lifelong learning.

**20. Name the four additional attributes.**
Project management skills, decision-making under constraints, social and environmental responsibility, leadership qualities.

**21. What is meant by decision-making under constraints?**
Balancing cost, time, quality and safety when no option satisfies all of them fully.

**22. Name the four core functions of a practicing engineer.**
Design and development; analysis and testing; implementation and operation; maintenance and support.

**23. What does the function 'analysis and testing' cover?**
Evaluating performance and ensuring quality.

**24. What does 'maintenance and support' ensure?**
Long-term functionality of the system over its life.

**25. What does technical proficiency mean?**
Mastery of core engineering principles.

**26. Why is teamwork listed as a key attribute?**
Engineers work in multidisciplinary teams with architects, doctors, software developers, government bodies and clients.

### Engineer as problem solver, designer and change agent

**27. What is meant by 'problem-solving' in the context of engineering?**
A systematic process of identifying a problem, studying existing solutions, requirements and constraints, then generating, designing, testing and launching a solution. It follows the Engineering Method.

**28. Name the six phases of the Engineering Method.**
Idea, Concept, Planning, Design, Development, Launch.

**29. What happens in the Concept phase?**
Existing solutions, requirements and constraints are studied.

**30. What happens in the Development phase?**
Prototypes, experiments, validation and verification.

**31. Name the six steps of the Scientific Method.**
Ask a question, do background research, construct a hypothesis, test the hypothesis, analyse data and draw a conclusion, communicate.

**32. State one difference between the Engineering Method and the Scientific Method.**
The scientific method seeks to explain a phenomenon and ends in knowledge. The engineering method seeks to build a solution under constraints and ends in a working product.

**33. What is the engineering design process?**
A series of steps engineers follow to find a solution to a problem: determining objectives and constraints, prototyping, testing and evaluation.

**34. Who is a change agent?**
Someone who identifies areas for improvement, initiates innovation, implements new solutions, and influences others toward positive transformation.

**35. Name the six key aspects of the engineer as a change agent.**
Technological transformation, solving societal challenges, sustainable development, economic growth, digital innovation, disaster management and recovery.

**36. Give one example of engineers driving technological transformation.**
Electrical engineers helped shift energy generation from fossil fuels to solar and wind power.

**37. Give one example of engineers solving societal challenges.**
Engineers Without Borders designs low-cost water purification systems for rural areas.

**38. Give one example of engineers in disaster management.**
Structural engineers redesign buildings to be earthquake-resistant.

## 5-mark questions

**Q1. Differentiate between engineering as a discipline and engineering as a profession.**

Engineering combines physics, mathematics and material science with innovation, analysis and design to meet human needs safely, efficiently and sustainably. The same field is viewed two ways: as a **discipline**, a body of knowledge, and as a **profession**, a career path.

| Aspect | Discipline | Profession |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Generation of knowledge, theories, innovation, research | Practical application of knowledge to real problems |
| Setting | Universities, research labs, academic institutions | Industry, consultancy, government agencies |
| Output | Scientific theories, experimental results, conceptual designs | Functional products, infrastructure, processes, services |
| Goal | Advance the frontiers of science and technology | Ensure safety, efficiency and service to society |
| Regulation | Not licensed | Licensed and regulated: PE in the US, Chartered Engineer via IEI in India |
| Example | A CS student studying algorithms and AI | A software engineer building secure banking applications |

The **discipline** rests on six components: scientific foundation, mathematical modelling, design thinking, technical knowledge, experimentation and analysis, innovation and research.

The **profession** rests on six elements: application of knowledge, professional responsibility, ethics and integrity, licence and certification, teamwork and communication, lifelong learning.

Conclusion: the discipline generates knowledge and the profession applies it. Engineering bridges theory and practice, being a discipline grounded in science and a profession rooted in service.

**Q2. Explain the key components of engineering as a discipline.**

Engineering as a discipline is a branch of academic study and systematic knowledge applying scientific and mathematical principles to solve real-world problems. Its characteristics are an interdisciplinary nature, a strong base in mathematics and science, an emphasis on design, analysis and experimentation, and a commitment to lifelong learning.

Six components:

| Component | What it means |
|---|---|
| Scientific foundation | Understanding the laws of nature, how forces act, how energy flows and how materials behave, drawn from physics, chemistry and biology |
| Mathematical modelling | Using equations to model systems, predict outcomes and optimise performance |
| Design thinking | Defining a problem, brainstorming, modelling and simulating, then improving through iteration |
| Technical knowledge | The specialised core of each branch, mechanical, electrical, civil, chemical or computer |
| Experimentation and analysis | Validating theory through labs, collecting and analysing data |
| Innovation and research | New materials, energy-efficient systems, improved manufacturing, advancing technology |

*In practice:* a mechanical student learns thermodynamics and machine design, an electrical student studies circuit theory and signal processing, and a computer science student explores algorithms, AI and data structures.

**Q3. Explain the key elements of engineering as a profession.**

The profession is the practical application of engineering knowledge to design, develop, operate and maintain systems that serve society, done responsibly, ethically and efficiently. It is licensed and regulated by bodies such as IEEE, ASCE and NEC.

1. **Application of knowledge** — using academic training in construction, manufacturing, energy, healthcare, IT and transport. Example: a mechanical engineer designing an energy-efficient HVAC system for a hospital.
2. **Professional responsibility** — decisions affect people, property and the environment, so engineers are trusted to act in the public interest. Example: a structural engineer ensuring a building is safe and compliant.
3. **Ethics and integrity** — following a code of ethics based on honesty, accountability and fairness, avoiding conflicts of interest and prioritising safety over profit. Example: refusing to certify a faulty design under management pressure.
4. **Licence and certification** — the PE licence in the US, the Chartered Engineer registration with IEI in India, needed to legally sign off on critical work.
5. **Teamwork and communication** — working in multidisciplinary teams. Example: a biomedical engineer designing a knee implant with surgeons and explaining the choices clearly.
6. **Lifelong learning and innovation** — continuously upgrading skills. Example: an electrical engineer learning AI and IoT for smart energy grids.

**Q4. Discuss the essential attributes of a practicing engineer.**

A practicing engineer applies engineering principles to real-world problems and is actively involved in the profession, either as a licensed PE or while gaining experience under a PE's supervision. The scope of the role is from concept to reality, transforming ideas into practical solutions.

*Six key attributes:*

| Attribute | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Technical proficiency | Mastery of core engineering principles |
| Ethical integrity | Adheres to codes of ethics and professional conduct |
| Problem-solving skills | Analytical and innovative mindset |
| Communication skills | Effective oral and written communication |
| Teamwork and collaboration | Works well in multidisciplinary teams |
| Adaptability and lifelong learning | Keeps up with technological advancement |

*Four additional attributes:*

| Attribute | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Project management skills | Planning, executing and leading projects |
| Decision-making under constraints | Balancing cost, time, quality and safety |
| Social and environmental responsibility | Understanding sustainability and societal impact |
| Leadership qualities | Influencing, guiding and mentoring others |

*Example:* the Challenger engineers had the technical proficiency to identify the O-ring risk. The failure lay in ethical courage and communication, since management overruled their concerns. This shows why the non-technical attributes matter as much as the technical ones.

**Q5. Explain the core functions of a practicing engineer.**

Four core functions run in sequence over the life of a system:

| Function | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Design and development | Creating products, processes or systems |
| Analysis and testing | Evaluating performance and ensuring quality |
| Implementation and operation | Overseeing production and daily functions |
| Maintenance and support | Ensuring long-term functionality |

Design and development → analysis and testing → implementation and operation → maintenance and support.

*Example, an automotive ECU:* the engineer designs the circuit and firmware, then tests it under vibration and thermal stress, then oversees its production line integration, then supports it in the field through diagnostics and firmware updates.

Each function maps onto later engineering topics: design and testing connect to design for safety and reliability, implementation to quality and productivity management, and the whole cycle to project management.

**Q6. Explain the Engineering Method and compare it with the Scientific Method.**

| Phase | Engineering Method | Activities | Scientific Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Idea | Identify the problem | Ask a question |
| 2 | Concept | Existing solutions, requirements, constraints | Do background research |
| 3 | Planning | Define objectives, plan the programme and schedule | Construct a hypothesis |
| 4 | Design | Drawings, schematics, models, algorithms, proof of concept | Test the hypothesis |
| 5 | Development | Prototypes, experiments, validation and verification | Analyse data and draw a conclusion |
| 6 | Launch | Results | Communicate |

**Comparison.** Both are systematic, evidence-based and iterative, and both begin by framing a question or problem. The difference is in purpose and output: the scientific method seeks to *understand and explain* a phenomenon, ending in knowledge, while the engineering method seeks to *build a solution* under given constraints of cost, time and safety, ending in a working product. Science asks *why*; engineering asks *how, within limits*.

**Q7. Why is a structured approach important in solving engineering problems?**

A structured approach means following the Engineering Method rather than jumping to a solution.

Reasons it matters:
- **Correct problem definition.** The Idea phase forces you to identify what the actual problem is, so effort is not spent solving the wrong one.
- **Constraints surface early.** The Concept phase captures requirements and constraints before design commits resources, and changes are cheaper the earlier they are made.
- **Objectives become measurable.** Planning sets objectives and a schedule, so progress can be tracked.
- **Verification is built in.** Development includes validation and verification, so failures are caught before launch rather than in the field.
- **Repeatability and documentation.** A structured process leaves a record others can review, audit and learn from.

*Counter-example:* the Hyatt Regency walkway collapse followed a design change that skipped re-verification. Structure exists precisely to prevent that. Data-driven decision-making strengthens the same process by replacing intuition with evidence at each phase.

**Q8. Explain the role of the engineer as a change agent.**

A change agent identifies areas for improvement, initiates innovation, implements new solutions, and influences others toward positive transformation.

| # | Aspect | What engineers do | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Technological transformation | Introduce new technologies such as AI, renewable energy and EVs | Electrical engineers driving the shift from fossil fuels to solar and wind |
| 2 | Solving societal challenges | Design systems for clean water, climate and healthcare access | Engineers Without Borders and low-cost rural water purification |
| 3 | Sustainable development | Promote eco-friendly design and practice | Green buildings, biodegradable materials, energy-efficient transport |
| 4 | Economic growth | Efficient products and systems raise productivity | Automation revolutionising global supply chains |
| 5 | Digital innovation | Create smart systems and AI-based solutions | Health apps that monitor and predict disease |
| 6 | Disaster management and recovery | Design early warning, rescue and rebuilding systems | Earthquake-resistant building design |

Conclusion: engineers do not merely respond to society's needs, they reshape what is possible, which is why responsibility and ethics accompany the capability.

**Q9. Explain the engineer's three roles: problem solver, designer and change agent.**

*Problem solver.* The engineer follows the **Engineering Method**: Idea (identify the problem), Concept (study existing solutions, requirements and constraints), Planning (define objectives and schedule), Design (drawings, models, algorithms), Development (prototypes, validation and verification), Launch (results). It runs parallel to the six steps of the scientific method but ends in a product rather than knowledge.

*Designer.* The engineer follows the **engineering design process**: determining objectives and constraints, prototyping, testing and evaluation. The process is iterative, with each test feeding back into the design.

*Change agent.* Beyond building what is asked, the engineer identifies improvements, initiates innovation and influences others. Six aspects: technological transformation, solving societal challenges, sustainable development, economic growth, digital innovation, and disaster management.

These three roles are not sequential but simultaneous. While carrying out the four core functions of design, testing, operation and maintenance, the engineer solves, designs and drives change at the same time.

**Q10. In what ways must different engineering fields collaborate to solve modern problems?**

Modern problems are interdisciplinary and no single branch owns them.

- **Multidisciplinary teams are an element of the profession.** Engineers collaborate with architects, doctors, software developers, government bodies and clients, and communication skills are listed among the key attributes precisely for this reason.
- **Complex systems demand multiple domains.** An electric vehicle needs mechanical engineers for the drivetrain, electrical engineers for the battery and motor, computer engineers for the control software, and materials engineers for lightweight structures.
- **A smart grid** combines electrical power engineering, IoT and communication networks, data analytics, and civil infrastructure.
- **Healthcare devices** need biomedical, electronics and software engineers working with clinicians. A biomedical engineer designing a knee implant works directly with surgeons and must explain the design in plain terms.
- **Disaster resilience** needs structural, civil, environmental and software engineers together for warning systems and rebuilt infrastructure.

Failure to collaborate has cost: the Mars Climate Orbiter was lost because two engineering teams used different unit systems, and the Airbus A380 was delayed by misaligned software versions across national teams. Collaboration is therefore not a soft skill but a technical requirement.

**Q11. Should an engineer always follow instructions from management, even if unsafe? Discuss.**

No. The professional code places public safety above obedience.

- **Ethics and integrity** is one of the six elements of the profession. Engineers must avoid conflicts of interest, ensure transparency and prioritise safety over profits. An engineer must refuse to certify a faulty design even under pressure from management if it compromises safety.
- **Professional responsibility** means decisions affect people, property and the environment, and engineers are trusted to act in the public interest, not merely in the employer's interest.
- **The licence carries this duty.** A PE or Chartered Engineer signs off on technical documents personally, so the responsibility cannot be transferred upward to a manager.
- **The NSPE code** requires engineers to hold paramount the safety, health and welfare of the public.

*Case:* the Challenger disaster. Engineers raised concerns about the O-ring in cold weather and were overruled by management pressure to launch. Seven lives were lost. The lesson drawn is that engineers must speak up and document risks, and that communication between technical and managerial teams is vital.

The balanced position: an engineer should follow legitimate management direction on scope, cost and schedule, but the duty to the public overrides any instruction that creates unacceptable risk. The right response is to document the concern in writing, escalate formally, and refuse to certify if unresolved.

**Q12. How does engineering act as a bridge between theory and application?**

Engineering exists at the junction of the two.

- **The discipline generates knowledge; the profession applies it.** The discipline lives in universities and research labs producing theories and conceptual designs. The profession lives in industry, government and consultancy producing infrastructure and services. Engineering is the same field seen from both ends.
- **The components map onto the application.** Scientific foundation and mathematical modelling are theory; experimentation and design thinking convert them into testable artefacts; technical knowledge and innovation carry them into products.
- **Example:** thermodynamics is a body of theory. A mechanical engineer uses it, along with fluid-flow principles, to design an energy-efficient HVAC system for a hospital, which is an applied outcome serving real people.
- **The Engineering Method is the bridge itself.** It takes an idea, applies existing knowledge and constraints, designs and validates, and ends in a launched product. The scientific method stops at communicating findings; the engineering method continues to delivery.

Conclusion: engineering is a discipline grounded in science and a profession rooted in service, and engineers shape the future through responsible innovation.
